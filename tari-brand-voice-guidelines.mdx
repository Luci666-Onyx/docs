## 1. Brand Personality

If Tari were a person, they'd be the friend who works in crypto but never makes you feel behind for not knowing the jargon — someone who's genuinely excited about what they're building, explains it in plain terms, and never oversells what it can do. They're credible because they're precise, not because they're loud.

## 2. Voice Attributes

### Confident

- **We are**: direct about what Tari does and why it matters, willing to make a clear claim when we can back it up
- **We are not**: boastful, hype-driven, or prone to superlatives ("revolutionary," "the best," "a money machine") that we can't substantiate
- **This sounds like**: "Tari is a proof-of-work protocol that lets you mine XTM from your own computer."
- **This does NOT sound like**: "Turn your computer into a money machine — it's a revolution!"

### Measured / Credible

- **We are**: precise about numbers, sources, and mechanics; comfortable saying "it depends" when it does
- **We are not**: hedgy to the point of sounding unsure, or so technical that newcomers check out
- **This sounds like**: "Mining rewards vary based on your hardware and network conditions."
- **This does NOT sound like**: "Guaranteed rewards, no risk, ever."

### Newcomer-Friendly (without condescension)

- **We are**: patient, plain-spoken, willing to define terms the first time they appear
- **We are not**: dumbed-down, cutesy, or reliant on "even your grandma could do this"–style jokes
- **This sounds like**: "New to mining? Here's what happens when you press start."
- **This does NOT sound like**: "It's so easy your Grandma could do it."

### Grounded Optimism

- **We are**: genuinely enthusiastic about the technology and community, expressed through specifics rather than adjectives
- **We are not**: breathless, meme-first, or leaning on unverified social proof ("700,000+ people love Tari!")
- **This sounds like**: "Over 700,000 people are actively mining on Tari — here's how the network has grown."
- **This does NOT sound like**: "700,000+ people like you love Tari!"

## 3. Audience Awareness

**Primary**: People new to crypto and mining — curious, possibly skeptical, want to understand what they're getting into before they commit hardware or money.

**Secondary**: Crypto-native users evaluating Tari against other mining/blockchain options — they'll skim past hype and look for the technical substance.

**Implication**: Copy should work on two levels — a clear, jargon-light hook for newcomers, with technical accuracy underneath that a crypto-native reader won't wince at. Avoid choosing hype over precision to win the newcomer's attention.

## 4. Core Messaging Pillars

1. **Accessible mining** — Anyone with a computer can participate; no specialized rig required. _(Must always pair with honest expectations about rewards.)_
2. **Confidential by design** — Default privacy features, explained in terms of what they actually protect against, not just "keeps you safe."
3. **Open, community-built** — Open source, powered by contributors and miners, not a closed company product.
4. **Real rewards, real work** — Mining is framed as contributing computing power in exchange for XTM — not passive income or a guaranteed windfall.

**Hierarchy**: Accessibility leads (it's the hook), confidentiality and openness build credibility, "real rewards" sets honest expectations before anyone downloads anything.

## 5. Tone Spectrum

The voice stays confident and credible everywhere; tone shifts by context:

| Context | Tone Adjustment |
| --- | --- |
| Homepage / first impression | Warm and clear — confidence dialed up slightly to hook interest, still no unverified claims |
| Product / download pages | Precise and instructional — walk through what happens step by step |
| Financial/earnings language | Measured and specific — always paired with variability caveats |
| Community / social | Slightly more casual, but never at the expense of accuracy |
| Legal / risk disclosures | Formal, plain-language, no attempt at personality |

## 6. Style Rules

| Rule | Tari's Choice |
| --- | --- |
| Oxford comma | Yes |
| Headings | Sentence case |
| Contractions | Use (keeps it approachable) |
| Numbers | Numerals for 10+, spell out one through nine |
| Percent | Use "%" |
| Superlatives ("best," "only," "revolutionary") | Avoid unless directly substantiated with a named source or spec |
| Exclamation points | Rare — maybe one per page, never stacked |
| ALL CAPS | Avoid; use bold for emphasis instead |
| Testimonials/social proof | Only real, attributable users; disclose any compensated or affiliated relationship |

## 7. Terminology

### Preferred vs. Avoid

| Use This | Not This | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Mine XTM" / "earn XTM" | "make money," "money machine" | Frames it as work-for-reward, not passive income |
| "Rewards vary" | "guaranteed rewards," "easy money" | Avoids implying a financial guarantee |
| "Confidential by default" + a specific mechanism | "keeps you safe" | Vague safety claims read as marketing fluff and carry compliance risk |
| "New to mining" | "even your Grandma could do this" | Same accessibility message, without an age-based cliché |
| "Active miners" (with a defined metric) | "people who love Tari" | Keeps social proof factual and sourced |

### Names & Endorsements

- Never list a person's name under "Supported by" without a documented relationship (investor, advisor, user) on file.
- Any paid or affiliated endorsement gets a visible disclosure, per FTC-style guidance for crypto promotion.
- Only feature testimonials from verifiable accounts — no parody-adjacent or unconfirmed handles.

## 8. Legal/Compliance Defaults (apply regardless of channel)

- Pair any earnings or reward language with a variability disclaimer (hardware, energy cost, network difficulty).
- Avoid absolute safety/security claims; describe the actual protective mechanism instead.
- Cite the source for any user-count or growth statistic.
- Disclose material relationships behind any name, logo, or quote used as social proof.
